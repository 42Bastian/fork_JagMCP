# The 68000: where Moira is corrected, and what is left out

The 68000 is Moira with its bus cycles made on the stepped bus
([0013](../decisions/0013-the-68000-is-moira.md)). Moira gives each instruction's bus cycles and
the clocks between them; the simulation's 68000 (the nuked 68000, `rtl/nuked-68k/68k.v`) is the
reference for both. Where they differ the subclass (`M68000`) corrects the cycles Moira makes, in the
times Moira gives them; Moira itself is not changed.

## The bus cycles corrected

Each was found by a reference that differed, and is checked by `random_m68k` and `tests/m68k`.

| Where | The simulation's 68000 | Moira |
|---|---|---|
| Reset | two clocks after the first fetch of the program before the second | none |
| STOP | no fetch of the word after its operand | fetches it, in the same time |
| DBcc whose count ends | its dummy read is of the word at the branch's target | of the word after the displacement |
| ANDI, ORI, EORI and MOVE to CCR or SR | the dummy read fetches the word at PC again, in program space | the word after it (ANDI and its kin in data space) |
| TAS of memory | one bus cycle: AS held low from the read to the end of the write, no strobes between | a read and a write |
| ADD, SUB, ADDQ, SUBQ, ADDI, SUBI of a long word in memory; MOVEM of long words to memory predecremented | the low half written first | the high half first |
| JSR | the target's first word fetched before the return address is pushed | after it |
| MOVEM of long words from memory | the extra read is of the word after the last, at the end, in the operands' space (program space from a PC relative address) | of the first word, before the others, in data space |
| ADDX and SUBX of long words predecremented | each operand's low half read first | its high half first |
| ROXL and ROXR by a register's count of zero | C cleared | C set to X, as the manual has it |
| The acknowledge of an interrupt | both strobes; Tom ends it in a cycle and gives vector 64 | Moira's autovector; here a bus cycle of function code 7, its S4 two clocks before Moira asks for the vector |

## Interrupts

Tom's `intl` is the 68000's IPL (level 2). Where the 68000 decides on an interrupt does not follow
Moira's polling, so the subclass sets the level Moira compares with the mask at each instruction
boundary itself: Tom's line four cycles before the S0 of the instruction's last program read, as it
was asked for (a start held back by the bus's arbitration does not move it). While stopped, the
line ten cycles back: as it stops if the mask let the interrupt through before STOP loaded SR,
else once it has been stopped eight cycles (`m68k_jerry_irq`, `m68k_irq`). Woken by the vertical
interrupt the simulation's 68000 takes 43 to 49 cycles from the line's rise to its acknowledge,
by a phase this rule does not follow: the core's can be two cycles early.

This rule is what most instructions show, not all. Where the simulation's 68000 decides earlier, it
takes the interrupt an instruction later than the core: CMPM, MOVEP, MOVEM from memory, MOVE to
an address register postincremented, ADDX and SUBX predecremented, among those seen. The deciding
point there is not the same cycle of the instruction from one case to the next; it would take the
68000's microcode to place it. A reference with interrupts is therefore hand-written and checked to
agree (`m68k_irq`); generated programs with interrupts are not references.

## Left out

- **Address errors.** The simulation's 68000 starts a bus cycle with AS and no strobes, pushes its
  group 0 frame in another order than Moira, and stacks the complement of the address (`$FFFFBF72`
  for `$408D`), which the 68000 does not. Programs do not rely on them; none is a reference.
- **The data bus's high byte in the acknowledge cycle** is the bus's: the trace leaves it out in both
  (`D=--40`).
- **Tom's data bus** does not take what the 68000 reads or writes (it holds the lanes of the other
  masters' cycles, [0012](../decisions/0012-blitter-and-tom-data-bus.md)).
- **Jerry's registers** read by the 68000, but for the timers, the joystick ports, JINTCTRL and the
  DSP's space, are pulled up (`$FFFF`); written, but for the timers, JOYSTICK, JINTCTRL, the
  EEPROM's chip selects and the DSP's space, they go nowhere.
- **A RISC's CTRL and FLAGS read by the 68000 while the RISC runs**: their high half is the
  pipeline's state in the cycle the RISC serves the read (the queue, the gateway, the scoreboard).
  The core keeps it for the cycles the RISC makes while a 68000 cycle into its space is under
  way; where the RISC has run past the read before that cycle started (a stall it took at once,
  a node of the automaton), the read gets the RISC's state as it stands. A check leaves the data of
  such a read out ([spec/trace.md](../spec/trace.md)); what the 68000 then does with it (the BIOS
  keeps it in a register it later pushes) can still differ. Once the RISC has stopped they read as
  the simulation's: GO clear, the queue empty.
- **The cycle a slave access takes from the RISC** (`ioreq`, holding the RISC's own data and
  program requests back) is given up by the GPU for the 68000's and the blitter's cycles and by the
  DSP for reads of its space (two cycles after Tom's I/O cycle starts, the word read in the cycle
  after); the 68000's writes into the DSP's space take it as Jerry's slave passes them on. One the
  RISC has run more than a cycle past is lost. While the 68000 reaches for any of Jerry's
  registers the DSP steps with the bus, as a timer it writes may interrupt it at once; and with a
  68000 program a RISC skipping a waiting loop does not skip past the part of the run under way.
- **The DSP fetching code from outside its local RAM** (a jump there) is not modelled; the GPU's
  fetches there are ([architecture.md](../architecture.md)).
